Inside a Commission

Fairmont Hanoi

What does it mean to make art in a city a thousand years deep? The Fairmont Hanoi commission demanded we find out, through archives, craft villages, and a medium asked to do more than it usually does.

01
Lacquer Histories

The Heritage of Thăng Long

Thăng Long, the rising dragon, is the ancient name of Hanoi, given by Emperor Lý Thái Tổ when he established it as the imperial capital in 1010. Before commissioning a single piece, we spent weeks in archives: bronze drums, ceramics of the Lý and Trần courts, lacquered altar pieces of the Lê dynasty workshops.

Historical grounding is what gives art its weight. The lacquerwork tradition of the Red River Delta carries a lineage traceable to at least the fifteenth century. We did not borrow from it. We studied it, then gave the artisans a brief worthy of what they already knew.

02
Preserving Craftsmanship

Working with the villages

The lacquerwork was produced in our workshop in Vietnam, where technique is passed on through apprenticeship. These are working studios: noisy, practical places where the smell of lacquer resin is constant.

Twenty or thirty layers, each sanded before the next: it takes a career to master. Our relationship with these workshops is collaborative: we arrive knowing what the workshop is genuinely capable of, and we stay through the process. The result is work that could not be made anywhere else, at any price.

03
Modernity & Luxury

Mixed media, challenged tradition

Lacquer on metal. Lacquer into deeply carved wood at a complexity classical practice would not have attempted. Works interrupted by bronze, copper, and eggshell, in deliberate tension between what the medium is and what it has been asked to do. The result carries the depth of classical Vietnamese lacquerwork in a language that is entirely contemporary.

How we work

From first brief to final handover

One team carries each commission from the first site visit to the last piece on the wall, with clear documents at every stage for your design, procurement and operations teams.

01

Brief & site study

We walk the site, read the drawings, and listen to the design team and the operator. From this we shape an art strategy for the property: its narrative, its zones, its budget and its timeline.

Why it mattersOne direction for design, operations and budget.
02

Concept & curation

A concept presentation for every area, with artists, mediums and placements, refined with you until it is approved.

Why it mattersA clear vision to review, refine and approve.
03

BOQ & specification

Every piece is itemised in a bill of quantities and documented in a specification book, with dimensions, materials, framing and fixing, ready for budgeting or tender.

Why it mattersA sound basis for pricing, tender and procurement.
04

Production & quality control

Works are made by our artists, partner workshops and our own workshop. Our team inspects each piece at the workshop before it leaves for site.

Why it mattersEvery piece arrives on site as approved.
05

Installation & handover

We install and sign off each area with your team, then hand over a complete record of the collection: a schedule of every piece as installed, with its location, materials and dimensions.

Why it mattersA clear record for the team who will care for the collection.
