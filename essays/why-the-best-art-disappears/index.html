From the Studio

Why the Best Art Disappears

On Collaboration 2026
Theme Art, collaboration & the hospitality interior
Medium Cross-disciplinary practice
Year 2026

When we walk into a hotel space for the first time, we try to arrive with nothing to say. We are not there to assess or to plan yet, only to listen to what the room is already saying before we have decided anything about it.

A finished space is never the work of one hand. By the time a guest walks in and feels something (calm, arrival, intimacy, ease), many people have already shaped that feeling. The architect who set the proportions. The interior designer who chose the materials and the light. The operator who understands, better than anyone, who will move through the space and what they will need from it.

Art arrives into a conversation already underway. This is something we hold close at HOATAY: we are not the first voice in a space, and we are not the loudest. We are often the last, so our work begins by listening.

Listening first

Before we propose anything, we spend time understanding what others have already set in motion. We walk the space at different hours when we can, because light changes a room entirely: what reads as calm in the morning can feel cold by afternoon. We notice where people slow down and where they pass through without looking up. We pay attention to how sound moves, where the eye naturally rests, what the architecture is reaching for beneath what is immediately visible.

We ask what the designer intended by this material, this restraint, this particular quality of stillness. We ask what the operator needs the room to hold at seven in the morning and again at midnight, because the same space must do very different things across a single day.

These are not questions we ask in order to overrule. They are questions we ask because everything that follows depends on the answers. The most useful thing an art consultant can do is arrive without ego, curious about what is already present, rather than eager to announce what they have brought.

The thread that ties

Art, placed well, is connective tissue. It is the thread that draws the architect's structure, the designer's surfaces, and the operator's purpose into a single, coherent feeling. Done poorly, art competes: it announces itself, pulls the eye away, joins a different conversation from the one the room is having. Done well, it completes the room so naturally that a guest cannot quite say why, only that something is right.

Restraint is part of this. Sometimes the most connective act is to add very little, or to leave something deliberately open. A wall that stays bare is not a wall we forgot. It is a breath the room needed. Knowing what not to place is the same skill as knowing what to place, and we try to hold both with equal care.

This is why we think of ourselves less as authors than as collaborators. The work that lasts is many hands in accord, making something none of them could have made alone.

A wider circle

But a place is larger than its rooms. A guest has not only arrived somewhere comfortable; they have arrived somewhere: a city, a coastline, a culture with its own hands and history. The most considered hotels understand that a stay is also a kind of introduction to a place.

This is where art does something that furniture cannot. A chair, however beautiful, gives comfort and form. A work of art carries the hand that made it: a particular person, a tradition, a material drawn from that ground. A small object, holding more than its size.

So our collaboration does not end with the design team. It reaches outward to the artists and makers of the place itself. We work with local hands wherever we can, because a piece made nearby connects the hotel to its destination, and the guest to the community whose culture they have, however briefly, come to be among.

And when we say yes to a maker, we are carrying their work into a space they may never enter themselves. Their hands will be present in a room where guests may not know their name, may not think about the studio or workshop where this was built, layer by careful layer. We hold that responsibility with care. It is one of the reasons the placing matters as much as the choosing.

Art becomes an introduction between the traveller and the place, and a way for the people of that place to remain present within it.

In good company

We are sometimes asked what makes a HOATAY project recognisable. The honest answer is that the best ones are not recognisable as ours at all. The art looks as though it grew from the same intention as everything around it, as though the architect, the designer, the operator, the artist, and the place itself had all been in the same room from the beginning.

This is why the best art disappears. It belongs so completely that you cannot imagine the space without it, or quite see where it ends and the room begins. It has stopped being an artwork placed inside a space and become part of what that space is, something that was always going to be there.

That is the whole ambition: for the space, and the city around it, to speak with one voice before anyone notices the art. And for that voice to sound as though it could only have come from here.
